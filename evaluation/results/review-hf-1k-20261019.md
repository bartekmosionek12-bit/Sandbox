## Label provenance

No label in this evaluation comes from a safeloadai verdict. Each label comes from one
of two sources:

- the Hugging Face Hub's own scanners, read through `HfApi.get_paths_info(expand=True)`:
  - the pickle-import scan, with each import's safety;
  - the antivirus scan message;
  - the file's security status;
- a hand review of that evidence together with the repo and file name.

`fetch_malicious_hf.proposed_label` applies the rule to the Hub's evidence only. Every
row of `evaluation/hf_malicious_labels.tsv` was then reviewed by hand, and each row
gives its source in the reason column. The file has no reason that cites safeloadai
(`grep -ci safeloadai` gives 0).

- **The 49 malicious hf-malicious files.** 27 are labelled from a Hub pickle-scan sink
  import, 8 from a Hub antivirus signature other than the `__main__` one, and 14 by
  hand. The hand labels are files with Hub status "unsafe" but no per-file detail in
  the API, whose repo and file name declare an exploit or test payload.
- **The 4 relabelled files from the benign corpus.**
  - `etwithin/aws_sdk_pandas-ace-poc/malicious.pkl` is malicious from the Hub pickle
    scan (`posix.system`) and its antivirus signature.
  - `hfscanner/model/lambda_attack.h5` is malicious by hand: Hub status "unsafe", in
    a scanner test repo, with a file name that declares an attack.
  - The two `tulayaka/test-x3` files are "unclear". Their Hub status is "suspicious"
    with no detail, and that alone settles nothing, so they count in no rate. The
    `os.system` safeloadai reports in their config is mentioned above only as an
    observation. It did not set the label.
- **The 2 `ScanMe/test-models/eval_lambda` files in the tune half.** They are also in
  the hf-malicious set, and they are among the 14 hand labels above.
